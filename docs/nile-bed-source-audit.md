# Nile Bed — source and asset audit

**Audit date:** 18 October 2026. **Status:** Public page text was reviewed in the connected browser. User-supplied ownership and business details remain attributed to the user. No factory/product image was added to the site; request company-approved originals before treating any photography as a company asset.

## Sources reviewed

- The public [Nile Bed Facebook business page](https://www.facebook.com/p/Nile-Bed-100047775297291/) was accessible. It describes the business as a wholesaler of mattress materials and publicly lists 010 90000676 and info@nilebed.com. A post dated 4 December 2025 mentions an imported memory-foam shipment and multiple heights. This is evidence of a dated post, not current stock or a fixed product specification.
- The public [Nile Bed Facebook profile, ID 100009326443352](https://www.facebook.com/profile.php?id=100009326443352) was accessible and showed a Nile Bed profile based in Cairo. A visible post described imported medical/mask fabric. This was excluded from the mattress catalog because its current relevance to the mattress business could not be established.
- The public [Atef Taha Facebook profile](https://www.facebook.com/tefa.elsid) was accessible. A visible post mentioned memory foam from a Turkish source, in 2 cm and 4 cm heights. The user identified Atef Taha as the owner; that ownership claim is not independently established by the profile. An unrelated garment-factory employer entry was not used.
- The machinery supplier's official [VTC 100 page](https://www.veyselkutuklu.com/en/machines/vtc-series-automatic-bonnell-spring-transfer-machine/) was accessible and names VTC Series, Bonnell spring units, nominal performance of 80 springs per minute and stated capacity of 38,400 springs per 8 hours. These figures describe the supplier's machine, not verified Nile Bed equipment or capacity. The [VKY page](https://www.veyselkutuklu.com/en/machines/vky-series-automatic-bonnell-spring-coiling-machine/) returned 404 when opened during this review.

## Asset inventory and rights

The earlier site used a local generic vector spring illustration at `public/nilebed-springs.svg`; this asset has since been removed from the hero/gallery so no unapproved spring image is presented as a company product. The Facebook pages show logos and posts/videos, but this pass did not save an original media file suitable for deployment. The machine images found in image search belong to Veysel Kütüklü and were not copied into the site or presented as Nile Bed equipment. Request the company’s original logo and approved product, raw-material, workshop and machine-line photos/videos, with permission for commercial website use.

## Website safeguards and remaining checks

The website now states the business scope explicitly and in both languages: Nile Bed is a wholesaler of mattress materials and supplies and manufactures springs; it does not manufacture or sell finished mattresses. That statement appears in the hero footnote, a dedicated scope section, the catalog, the about section, the page metadata and the Organization JSON-LD. The site lists the public email and WhatsApp contact link and describes the memory-foam detail only as information from a dated public update, with current availability and specifications subject to confirmation. See `docs/scope-audit-2026-10-18.md`. Its RFQ form remains disabled and does not submit or store information. No specific spring model, production capacity, product guarantee, factory address or Veysel machine ownership is claimed.

Before launch, confirm the preferred spelling/title for Atef Taha and permission to name him; identify exact product and supply categories; confirm which materials are sourced, traded or manufactured; confirm current memory-foam availability; and verify spring families, equipment models, capacity, dimensions, quality processes, MOQ, lead times and delivery. Obtain approved company imagery and rights, and configure/validate a secure RFQ destination before enabling form submission.

### Follow-up search for black/grey felt — 19 October 2026

A focused search for black mattress-insulation felt returned mostly adhesive furniture-leg pads, craft felt rolls, and generic recycled PET felt in multiple colors. None of the retrieved images confirms the exact mattress-grade black/grey felt supplied by Nile Bed, so they were not used as if they were the company product. Examples reviewed: [black felt-tape retail search](https://amazon.com/black-felt-tape/s?k=black+felt+tape), [craft felt insulation search](https://amazon.com/felt-insulation/s?k=felt+insulation), and [Estrella multi-color polyester felt](https://estrellafelt.com/product-category/polyester-felt). The catalog therefore keeps its explicitly illustrative visual of black felt plus three grey shades and labels it as illustrative pending owner-approved product photography.


## Homepage visual and catalog imagery refresh — 19 October 2026

The six user-supplied references guided a clean, light-first editorial redesign: generous white space, oversized typography, simple navigation, clear product cards, and restrained black/coral accents. The homepage hero now uses one clearly labeled AI-generated illustrative Bonnell spring-unit photo; the category cards use distinct, centered component/material illustrations. They are representative visuals, not Nile Bed product photography or verified stock. No finished mattress is shown or implied.

The catalog now places each SKU’s mapped illustration inside its own centered product card, using contain-fit rather than cropping imagery toward the edges. Product families share an image where the same component is offered in different sizes/specifications; frame and pocket components have distinct illustrations. The third-party Bonnell reference image was removed rather than reusing a supplier photo without clear reuse rights. Search results for other materials were mostly irrelevant, low-resolution marketplace thumbnails, or supplier-marked imagery, so they were not presented as Nile Bed products. All remaining new images are labeled illustrative pending owner-approved product photography.

The previous preview’s repeating CSS coil pattern was removed from the hero; it had visually competed with the products and did not match the supplied references. The hero is now image-led and the remaining sections retain the separate business propositions, bilingual copy, and truthful direct-supply versus partner-referral distinction.
